About

From first login to shipping in production

A technologist's path rarely runs in a straight line. Mine started with the basics — keyboards, file systems, the first "hello world" — and has stretched, year over year, toward the edge of what's trending in software today.

Software engineering & technical research, Freetown

I'm Joseph Lahai Kanu — a software engineer and technical research specialist working out of Freetown, Sierra Leone. My work sits at the intersection of teaching and building: I write the tools I wish someone had handed me when I was learning, and I document the path so the next person's climb is a little shorter.

Over eight years I've moved from foundational computer literacy through the full stack — front end, back end, developer tooling — and into the technologies that are actively reshaping how software gets made. That range shows up in what I build: browser-based code editors, data-driven learning tools, and small platforms for the communities around me.

I care about African tech having a seat at the table, not as a footnote but as a source of ideas. Freetown is where I work from — it's not incidental to the work.

Journey

Eight years, one throughline

Growth that compounds: each stage built the footing for the next.

Year 1–2

Foundations

Core computer literacy, file systems, and the first lines of HTML and CSS — the unglamorous groundwork that everything else stands on.

Year 3–4

Learning to build tools, not just pages

A shift from static sites to logic-driven applications — JavaScript, server-side rendering with EJS, and the beginning of a habit: building small dev tools like in-browser code editors to sharpen my own understanding.

Year 5–6

Full-stack and API-driven thinking

Working across the stack, consuming and designing APIs, and treating data — not just layout — as the core material of a good application.

Year 7–8

Technical research & trending technology

Moving from "can I build this" to "what's the right way to build this" — research-driven engineering, staying close to emerging tools instead of catching up to them late.

Now

CodeZerra

Building and researching at CodeZerra, with an eye on what African tech looks like when it's given room to lead rather than follow.
